Jobs are the heart of the Workshop. This article explains what a job and a run are, how work gets into the list and what to do when something needs attention. Open **Workshop > Jobs** to follow along.

## Jobs and runs

A **job** is one piece of work on one machine for one recipient, a member or an organization. It has a number such as J-2026-000001 and, if you like, a title. The recipient is who gets the result and is billed for it.

A job is made of one or more **runs**. A run is one period of work on the machine, for example one print or one laser cut. A reprint is simply another run of the same job.

## Manual and recorded runs

- A **manual** run is entered by a person. This is how work on machines that do not report anything is logged.
- A **recorded** run is reported by the machine itself. Printers with a connector send their runs, progress and material use automatically.

A job can mix both kinds. The job page shows the runs, the material used and the price.

## Getting work into the list

- **New job** in the toolbar logs work by hand. Choose the machine and the recipient, say whether the run starts now or is already done, and add the material. You are the operator unless you pick someone else.
- **Claim** turns a recorded run into part of a job. Runs that no job owns yet wait in the strip **Reported by machines, not assigned** above the table. Claim one to attach it to an open job on the same machine or to start a new job for a recipient.

If you attach a run to a job that has a manual run for the same work, you can replace the manual run. It stays as history and its material goes back to stock.

## Finding and reading jobs

Use the search field for names, numbers, machines and recipients. The filter button adds status, outcome, machine, process, billing, recipient and operator. Click a row to see the details next to the list, or open the job for the full page. On a narrow screen a click opens the page directly.

## Needs attention

The flag at the end of a row means something should be fixed, for example a failed run, no recipient, a missing outcome or an incomplete price. The **Needs attention** tab lists only those jobs.

## Deleting and the recycle bin

Removing a job or a run moves it to the **Recycle bin** tab, where it can be restored. Deleting for good is not possible once material of the job has been booked, because the stock history keeps its link. Only people with the permission to delete see these actions.
