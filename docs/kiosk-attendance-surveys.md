# Kiosk, attendance, session policies, and surveys

The entrance kiosk is an ordinary registered ManagedDevice with `terminal_enabled`, an explicit SessionPolicy, attendance assurance settings, check-out options (`allowTapCheckout`, `offerSurveysAtCheckout`, `kioskResetSeconds`), and declared hardware capabilities. The public `/kiosk` route accepts only a valid managed-device credential. It never consumes an existing staff Session, so public requests cannot inherit staff permissions. Staff sign-in creates a Session bound to that device. Server authentication rejects that Session without the same active device identity, and idle or absolute expiry makes it unusable before the browser redirects to the policy destination.

Unregistered browsers use the one default SessionPolicy and are never inserted into `managed_devices`. Sessions snapshot the selected policy ID/version, idle interval, absolute deadline, post-session destination, and optional device ID. User activity extends idle expiry through `POST /auth/activity`, capped by the absolute deadline. Policy or device-policy changes revoke affected active Sessions transactionally.

Attendance is separate from authentication. Password or PIN verification at the kiosk returns a verified Person/Account/assurance result without creating a Session. Check-in locks the enabled Account and Person, prevents two open Visits, and evaluates the person's Lab Rules state. When they must confirm the current version, the check-in without a decision answers 409 `lab_rules_confirmation_required` (role in `blocking` mode) or `lab_rules_confirmation_suggested` (role in `warning` mode) and records nothing. The kiosk then shows the current version (`GET /terminal/lab-rules`, `GET /terminal/lab-rules.pdf`, device-authenticated; only the current effective version is ever served) with a checkbox and repeats the check-in with `confirmLabRulesVersionId`: the confirmation (method `kiosk`) and the admitted Visit commit in one transaction, and an id that is no longer the current version answers 409 `lab_rules_version_outdated`. A `warning` role may choose "Later" (`deferLabRules: true`) and is admitted with admission decision `warning`. A supervised check-in of a `blocking` person without the current confirmation answers 409 `lab_rules_confirmation_required` (they confirm themselves at the kiosk or in the app); `warning` roles are admitted with a warning. Supervised operations use registered attendance permissions; voiding requires `attendance.correct`, an optimistic version, and a reason.

Completing visitor enrollment on the kiosk counts as arriving, in the enrollment transaction (check-in method `enrollment`, assurance `low`, checked in by the new account, on the enrollment device). New visitors carry no initial role of their own: the database gives every new person the Visitor membership role (see [staffing-and-dashboard.md](staffing-and-dashboard.md#membership)), and enrollment grants nothing else. When the Visitor role asks for the Lab Rules, the visitor must tick "I have read and accept the Lab Rules" (`labRulesAccepted`, 422 otherwise); enrollment records the confirmation (method `enrollment`) of the pinned version and checks them in with admission `admitted`. If another version became current meanwhile, the submission is refused with 409 `lab_rules_changed` and the kiosk starts over. The enrollment result reports `attendance: checked_in`; the visitor does not check in again.

## Visits are anonymized at check-out

A visit keeps its person only while the person is in the lab: the open visit is what presence, check-out and the Lab Rules admission need. Every check-out (identified at the kiosk, tapping a name, by a supervisor) does this in its one transaction, after it raised the `check_out` notification trigger and created the post-visit survey invitation or the tablet's offer:

1. the visit is rolled into `attendance_daily_statistics` (UTC days): visitor count and unique visitor on the check-in day, seconds per day it spans, completed count and duration on the check-out day;
2. `people.last_visit_on` is set to the UTC date of the check-in (only the date of the last completed visit is kept per person, no history);
3. the visit row is deleted.

A person counts as a unique visitor of a day unless their `last_visit_on` already is that day. Peak occupancy is recorded as a candidate after every check-in and before every check-out (the number of open visits at that moment). Voiding deletes an open visit; it adds nothing to the statistics. `GET /visits` therefore lists only open visits, and `GET /attendance/statistics` sums the daily statistics of every day the period touches and adds the open visits live; `uniqueVisitors` is the sum of the daily unique visitors. Migration 00129 rolled the completed and voided visits that existed then into the statistics, filled `last_visit_on` and deleted them.

Survey invitations and tablet offers keep a random check-out reference in `visit_id` (not the visit's ID, which appears in URLs and logs) only to match the invitation and the offer of one check-out; an open offer carries `person_id` for its 15 minutes, cleared when it is consumed and deleted with the offer when it expires.

**Audit.** Ordinary check-ins and check-outs (kiosk, tap, enrollment, supervised check-in) write no audit event: while the person is here the visit is the record, and afterwards nothing personal is kept. Only corrections by the team are audited, `visit.supervised_check_out` (someone checked out another person) and `visit.voided`, with the acting account but without the visit's ID, the person or the reason. For these corrections the rule that a mutation and its audit event commit in one transaction still holds; check-ins and check-outs have no audit event to commit. Migration 00129 deleted the earlier `visit.checked_in`, `visit.checked_out`, `visit.public_checkout` and `visit.supervised_check_in` events and removed the visit ID from the earlier corrections.

Public presence exposes only first name plus surname initial and Visit ID. The kiosk refetches presence every 10 seconds. Which ways of identifying a kiosk takes for checking in and out is set per device (below). Checking out by tapping a name in the presence list is an additional option, available only when the device has `allowTapCheckout` (otherwise `POST /terminal/presence/{visitId}/check-out` answers 403); the former `checkoutMode` (`verified`/`public_tap`) was replaced by this switch, and migration 00119 turned every `public_tap` device into `allowTapCheckout = true`. Completed visits are not kept (see [Visits are anonymized at check-out](#visits-are-anonymized-at-check-out)); interactive statistics are limited to a 90-day range.

Surveys (drafts, conditional questions, share links, post-visit invitations, results and CSV export) are documented in [surveys.md](surveys.md). Checkout creates eligible invitations in the check-out transaction, before the visit is deleted.

The web bridge contract is versioned and selected at runtime. The Tauri shell injects a private loopback bridge configuration; it reports capabilities and emit only technical credential events (`nfc`, `qr`, or `barcode`). The web application and Core own resolution and business rules. The current desktop implementation supports PC/SC NFC UID reads; concrete USB drivers and other hardware adapters remain separate deployment work.

## Kiosk device settings

A kiosk device (`managed_devices`) carries these attendance settings:

- `checkInMethods` and `checkOutMethods`: any combination of `email_code`, `pin` and `password` (stored in that order, which is also the order the kiosk offers them). Check-in needs at least one; check-out may be empty only while `allowTapCheckout` is on, so a kiosk always has a way out (422 otherwise, and a database check). The terminal endpoints refuse a method the device does not list with 403 `attendance_method_not_allowed` before any credential is checked; `POST /terminal/email-code` is refused unless `email_code` is listed for check-in or check-out. Migration 00133 replaced the former `checkInAssurance`/`checkOutAssurance` levels by the sets they accepted: `low` became all three, `normal` the password and the emailed code, the never reachable `strong` levels the password alone.
- `allowTapCheckout` (default false): also allow checking out by tapping a name in the presence list. When the device also lists check-out methods, the tap list offers "Check out with your sign-in instead".
- `offerSurveysAtCheckout` (default false): offer the check-out survey after a check-out at the tablet, self or tap (below).
- `kioskResetSeconds` (15 to 600, default 60): idle time after which the tablet returns to its start screen. `GET /terminal/context` returns all of these.

"Used as" (visitor kiosk, with staff, staff only) is a view over `allowedApplicationModes` and `terminalEnabled`; it has no field of its own.

## Check-out survey at the tablet

After a **self** check-out (password, PIN or emailed code; not a supervisor's check-out) on a device with `offerSurveysAtCheckout`, the check-out answer (`Visit`) carries `surveyOffer` when both of these hold: a published survey has an enabled `visit_checked_out` trigger with `askAtTablet`, and the survey's per-person cooldown allows it. The cooldown counts invitations and earlier tablet questions (`survey_ask_cooldowns`, written when an offer is made, so declining or answering also starts it). The trigger has no audience restriction today, so the offer goes to everyone who qualifies; there is no limit on the number of questions or on required free text.

`surveyOffer` holds `offerToken`, `surveyName`, `questionCount`, `anonymous`, `canAnswerNow`, `canSendByEmail` (the person has an e-mail address), `mailConsent` and `expiresAt`. `mailConsent` is true when the check-out's automatic invitation for this survey exists, that is the person granted the current Surveys & feedback consent and has an address, so the survey reaches them by e-mail anyway unless they answer at the tablet. The token is opaque, 15 minutes valid, single use, bound to the check-out, the person, the tablet and the survey version, and only its digest is stored (`survey_offers`; consuming an offer clears its check-out reference and person; expired offers are deleted by the survey delivery loop, about every minute, and before each new offer). The device-authenticated endpoints, which answer 404 for an unknown, used, expired or foreign-tablet token:

| Endpoint                                          | Effect                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| ------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `POST /terminal/survey-offers/{token}/answer-now` | Returns the questions (`PublicSurvey` shape). The offer stays valid.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| `POST /terminal/survey-offers/{token}/responses`  | Stores the answers like an invitation redemption: anonymous surveys keep no person and no visit link, identified surveys link the person. Consumes the offer and cancels the check-out's invitation, so the survey is not e-mailed as well.                                                                           |
| `POST /terminal/survey-offers/{token}/send-email` | Makes the invitation due now (bypassing the delay), or creates it, and consumes the offer, so one offer sends at most one e-mail. Works while the person's Surveys & feedback consent is off; the consent is not changed. 422 `no_email` without address. Audit `survey_offer.email_requested` (resource: the offer). |
| `POST /terminal/survey-offers/{token}/decline`    | "No thanks": consumes the offer, cancels the visit's invitation.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |

While an offer exists, the automatic e-mail invitation of that visit is postponed until the offer has expired. The automatic invitation (offered or not) is created only for people who granted the current text of the Surveys & feedback consent category.

The tablet shows **Answer now** and **Send by e-mail** (sends it right away) when `mailConsent` is true: "No thanks" would promise something the automatic e-mail does not keep, and the screen says that the survey otherwise comes by e-mail later. Without the consent it shows **Answer now**, **No thanks** and **Send by e-mail**, the explicit one-off request. Send by e-mail appears only with `canSendByEmail`.

### After a tap check-out

Anyone can tap any name, so the tablet cannot know who answers. On a device with `offerSurveysAtCheckout`, the answer of `POST /terminal/presence/{visitId}/check-out` may carry a `surveyOffer` under the same survey rules (the cooldown decides whether to ask). While it is open, the `survey_offers` row names the tapped person and the check-out reference like a self check-out offer, with `after_tap` set (migration 00136); using the offer clears both, and the delivery loop deletes it once it expires, so the link lasts at most 15 minutes. That is what lets the offer:

- send the survey by e-mail to the tapped person's own address on file (`canSendByEmail`, at most once per offer, audited like above), whatever the consent;
- report `mailConsent` and start the cooldown like a self check-out offer, and postpone the automatic invitation behind it;
- cancel the automatic invitation when the survey is answered at the tablet, so it is not e-mailed as well.

It reports `anonymous: true`. Its answers are always stored without a person (`identified_person_id` null), even for an identified survey: the response is written exactly like an anonymous answer after a self check-out, and the cancelled invitation keeps no person, reference or address, so nothing links the answer to the tapped person. `decline` only uses the offer up and leaves the tapped person's invitation alone, since a stranger's "No thanks" must not hold back an e-mail the person agreed to.

## Consent at the kiosk

The answer of a tablet check-in also carries `consentRequired` and `consentCategories` (`id`, `name`, `description`, `basis`) while the person has consent categories unanswered at their current text. The tablet posts the answers to `POST /terminal/consents` (`visitId`, `answers[{categoryId, granted}]`): they are stored with place `kiosk` and the tablet as for the app (audit `notification_consent.answered`), only for the person of an open visit that was checked in at this tablet within the last 10 minutes; every other case answers 404.

## Visitor enrollment steps

New visitors sign in with a code mailed to their verified address only: enrollment creates no password, no PIN and sends no invitation. The flow is name and photo, then email, then the code from the mail, then the notification consent and the Lab Rules:

1. **About you.** First and last name and, on kiosks that can take or upload one, a photo. There is no phone field.
2. **Email.** `POST /visitor-enrollment/email-verification` (`email`, enrollment context and CSRF header) binds the address to the enrollment context and mails it a six-digit code, valid for ten minutes and five wrong entries; a new request replaces the previous code and address. Delivery is synchronous: a malformed address answers `422 email_invalid`, a provider error `422 email_delivery_failed` (the kiosk asks for another address), disabled mail `503 mail_unavailable`, and an address that already is an account's login email `409 authentication_identifier_exists` (that person checks in instead). At most five send attempts are allowed per enrollment context, refused ones (such as an existing address) included, and five codes per address within fifteen minutes (`429 verification_rate_limited`). Only the code's HMAC digest is stored, on the enrollment context; audit `visitor_enrollment.email_code_sent` without address or code.
3. **Code.** `POST /visitor-enrollment/email-verification/confirm` (`code`) marks the address verified (`204`, audit `visitor_enrollment.email_verified`); a wrong, expired or exhausted code answers `422 challenge_invalid`. The enrollment state reports `email` and `emailVerified`.
4. **Messages.** The consent categories from the enrollment state (`consentCategories`, the categories with basis `consent`), each with a No/Yes switch; nothing is preselected and Next works with everything off. The submission sends an answer per category in `consents`; they are stored with place `kiosk` and the kiosk device in the enrollment transaction (audit `notification_consent.answered`), so the next check-in does not ask again. The step is left out when no category needs consent.
5. **Lab Rules**, only when the Visitor role asks for them (see below).

The submission carries no email: the account's login email is the address verified on the context, and without one the answer is `409 email_verification_required`. The new account's login email is verified and signs in with an emailed code (see [authentication](authentication.md#sign-in-with-an-emailed-code)); an address that became another account's login email meanwhile answers `409 authentication_identifier_exists`. Consuming the context clears the address from it.

The header links the imprint and privacy policy from the branding legal settings. On the kiosk and in visitor enrollment they open in a dialog on top of the page, because a kiosk has no way back from another website (the desktop app does not even open one): an internal document is shown as text, an external one in a frame with a QR code for reading it on a phone when the site refuses to be framed.

## Enrollment photo and on-screen keyboard

The "About you" step of visitor enrollment picks its photo source from the kiosk's effective capabilities (see [Managed devices](managed-devices.md)): `camera` shows "Start camera", `file_upload` shows the upload. After a photo is taken or chosen the visitor positions it under a round mask (drag, arrow keys, zoom slider 1x to 3x) and the visible square is uploaded as a 512x512 JPEG. With neither capability the photo is hidden and not required; the backend enforces this per device.

While the on-screen keyboard is open (the visual viewport is clearly smaller than the layout viewport and a text field has focus) the kiosk scrolls the focused field to the middle of the visible area and sizes its layout to the visible height, so the Next or submit button stays above the keyboard. The "About you" step then uses one column and hides the photo column and the progress header. The Lab Rules PDF modal is sized with `dvh` units to the visible screen; its pages scroll inside the modal while header and close button stay visible.

## Lab Rules in the frontend

- **App.** When the own status (`/auth/me` `laborordnungStatus`) has `actionRequired`, the application shell opens a modal with the current PDF (pdf.js, loaded lazily with the modal), an agreement checkbox and Confirm (`POST /laborordnung/confirmations/me`). In `blocking` mode it cannot be dismissed; in `warning` mode "Later" hides it until the page is loaded again (React state, nothing stored). The dashboard tile and notice link to `/lab-rules/confirm`.
- **`/lab-rules/confirm`.** Standalone page without navigation behind the session (signed-out people go to sign-in and return), the target of the "New Lab Rules" notification and e-mail. It shows the form, or "Nothing to confirm", "Already confirmed" or a no-published-version message, and a thank-you with a link into the app after confirming. A 409 `lab_rules_version_outdated` reloads the status and shows the new revision.
- **Kiosk.** A check-in answered with 409 `lab_rules_confirmation_required` (blocking) or `lab_rules_confirmation_suggested` (warning) opens the rules screen (`/terminal/lab-rules`, `/terminal/lab-rules.pdf`). "Confirm and check in" resubmits the same entries with `confirmLabRulesVersionId`; "Later" (warning only) resubmits with `deferLabRules`. The entries live only in that screen's state and are dropped on Back, after check-in and after 120 s without a touch.
- **Visitor enrollment.** The last step asks for "I have read and accept the Lab Rules" and sends `labRulesAccepted`; 409 `lab_rules_changed` reloads the state and returns to that step with a notice.
- **Staff.** Settings › Lab Rules › Confirmations lists who confirmed which revision and how (`laborordnung.confirmations.read`); the person page shows the latest five. The attendance page no longer has holds or a confirmation queue; assisted check-in of a blocking person who has not confirmed says they must confirm on the tablet or in the app first.
