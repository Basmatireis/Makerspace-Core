# Filament spools and generic printer integration

The filament and printing features extend the existing machine logbook, inventory, pricing, Machine Job, and Order model for one makerspace. They do not introduce a second stock total, billing path, scheduler, slicer, remote-control service, or manufacturer-specific printer protocol.

## Filament inventory

A gram-denominated filament article becomes spool-managed when it has a filament diameter. A **spool** is one filament unit (an original spool or a refill; which physical holder a refill sits in is not tracked): one article (`material_id`), one initial net weight, a remaining quantity, scan identifiers, mount state and a lifecycle `active` or `archived` (migration 00094 removed spool holders, fills, the reusable/single-use kind, changing filament and the empty spool). The remaining quantity is derived from the immutable `inventory_transactions` ledger by `filament_spool_remaining(spool)`, leaving deleted entries out. The material balance remains authoritative and reports assigned (on active spools) and unassigned (loose) quantities.

Registering a spool (`POST /filament-spools`, `filament_spools.manage` and `inventory.manage`) either books a purchase onto it (`new_acquisition`) or a zero-value `spool_transfer` from loose stock (`existing_stock`). Transfers between loose stock and spools or between two spools preserve the material totals. Consumption, physical corrections (weighing) and identifiers work on active spools only. Ledger entries and job usages keep the spool label as a snapshot next to the foreign key.

### Labels

The label (`displayId`) is `S-<TYPECODE>-<VARIANT>-<COLOUR>-<NN>` in upper case, built from the material type's `code`, the article's variant and colour name at the time the spool is registered, leaving missing parts out (`S-PLA-BASIC-DARK BLUE-01`). The label is not recomputed: renaming a variant leaves existing spool labels as they are. `NN` is the smallest number (two digits at least) that no **active** spool with the same prefix uses, so retiring a spool frees its number. Registrations lock the prefix for their transaction, and a partial unique index keeps labels unique among active spools; archived spools keep their label. Restoring a spool whose number was reused meanwhile gives it the next free number of its article's current prefix. Spools registered before migration 00094 keep their `S-YYYY-NNNNNN` IDs.

### Retiring, restoring and purging

- **Retire** (`POST /filament-spools/{id}/retire`, `{expectedVersion, outcome}`, `filament_spools.manage` and `inventory.manage`): `empty` books the remaining quantity as used up (`manual_consumption`, reason `unrecorded_consumption`), `discarded` as disposed (`disposal`, reason `disposed`). The spool is unmounted, its identifiers are retired, it is archived with `retiredOutcome`, `retiredQuantity` and the actor, and it appears in the inventory recycle bin.
- **Restore** (`POST /inventory/recycle-bin/spools/{id}/restore`, same permissions): the spool becomes active and unmounted; a discarded spool gets the written-off quantity back as a positive adjustment (reason `other`); identifiers come back unless another spool uses them.
- **Purge** (`DELETE /inventory/recycle-bin/spools/{id}/purge`, `filament_spools.manage` and `inventory.delete`): a genuine hard delete of a retired spool; identifiers and mount history go, ledger entries and run usages keep the label.

Spools carry `attention`: `remainder_low` while an active spool holds less than 10 % of its initial net weight, and `ran_out_during_run` while an active spool fed a segment of a reported line that was followed by a segment on another (or no) spool in the latest report of a run that is not deleted, and nobody has weighed or corrected it (an adjustment other than a job correction) or mounted it since the next segment started. The message names the run and Core's remainder. It is only a suggestion: the confirmed-empty unmount stays, and Core neither retires the spool nor books its remainder.

Material usage on a machine run distinguishes makerspace and external filament. Makerspace usage must reference an article and may reference an active spool; it consumes inventory and participates in material pricing. External usage carries a description, never references a spool and does not change inventory. Every usage records whether its quantity is actual or estimated.

## Filament articles

A filament **article** (a material whose type has kind `filament`) carries manufacturer, a variant (one of its type's variants), package size, storage location and colour itself and always has a filament diameter (1.75 mm by default); only filament articles can be put on spools (409 `material_not_filament`). Spools show their article's colour and manufacturer and never hold their own.

### Receiving filament

`POST /inventory/receipts` (see [machine-logbook.md](machine-logbook.md#receiving-stock)) places filament either in `storage` (loose stock) or on `new_spools` (one new spool per package, each with its own label, the total price split in whole cents with the remainder on the last spool). Spool placements also need `filament_spools.manage`. Identifiers are only accepted when exactly one spool is created.

### Inventory UI

The Inventory page (`/workshop/inventory?tab=materials|parts|spools|types|bin`) lists articles (filament and consumables under Materials, parts under Parts), spools, types and categories, and the inventory recycle bin. From 1056 px on, the articles and spools tables have a permanent detail panel: a row click (or the name link) selects the row into the panel (`?selected=`), which shows a fixed four-line head, the key facts, and compact tables of the article's active spools and latest ledger entries (or the spool's bookings); narrower screens open the article or spool page instead. Rows carry the API's `attention` flags as a warning icon at the far right. The article page shows figure tiles, the spools and the full ledger, the sale prices per pricing group (read-only, from `Material.prices`; they are set under Sales → Pricing, and the article form only links there; a price may come from the article, its variant or its type, see [Material rate resolution](machine-logbook.md#material-rate-resolution)), facts and notes. The article form picks the **variant** from a combo box of the chosen type's variants and also accepts a new name, which adds it to the type; parts have no variant field. The **Categories** table has a Variants column (searchable), and its category dialog lists the type's variants (add, rename, delete; a variant in use shows its article and rule counts and cannot be deleted) and, for types with machine use and with `pricing.read`, a **Rates per variant** table (variants × pricing groups, row "All variants" is the type's rate; a variant without a rate shows the inherited one). A new type gets its variants after it is saved. **Receive stock** places filament in storage or on new spools (one per package, labelled `S-<CODE>-<VARIANT>-<COLOUR>-NN`, numbered on saving). **Register spool** (spools tab, article page, or **Register** on a tag the printers detect but no spool carries) takes an article, the net weight and an optional RFID tag, from loose stock or as a purchase; from a detected tag it suggests the article (type code or name, variant from the sub-brand, nearest colour) and can mount the spool in the reported slot. A spool is **weighed** (gross minus tare, booked as an inventory count), mounted, unmounted, given identifiers and **retired** (ran empty or the rest was thrown away); retired spools, deleted articles (**Delete article**, `inventory.delete`) and deleted ledger entries (trash icon in the ledger, `inventory.ledger.delete`) are listed in the Recycle bin tab with **Restore** and **Delete permanently**, each offered only with its permission. Deleted jobs and runs stay in the jobs recycle bin, which the inventory bin links to. The detected-spools strip labels the suggestion **Match** only when the article's colour code is visually the same as the reported one (RGB distance at most 25 of 441) and **Similar colour** when it is merely close (at most 80); further away it offers a **New article**, and the reported colour is always shown next to the suggestion. A new article created from a detection is prefilled with type, variant, colour code and, when known, the colour name: the official Bambu Lab name of the product code the printer reads from the tag (`productCode`, catalogue in `frontend/src/features/machinelogbook/bambuColors.ts`), else the name of an article whose colour code equals the reported one. A spool is **weighed** (gross minus tare, booked as an inventory count), mounted, unmounted, given identifiers and **retired** (ran empty or the rest was thrown away); retired spools, deleted articles (**Delete article**, `inventory.delete`) and deleted ledger entries (trash icon in the ledger, `inventory.ledger.delete`) are listed in the Recycle bin tab with **Restore** and **Delete permanently**, each offered only with its permission. Deleted jobs and runs stay in the jobs recycle bin, which the inventory bin links to.

## Gated by process

Everything on this page exists for machines whose process has the **FDM printer** integration (`fdm_printer`, see [Processes](machine-logbook.md#processes)): the printer setup and wizard, connector assignments, spool inputs and mounting, artifacts, live status and reported grams. Other processes (integration `none`) have no printer setup; the backend answers `409 machine_not_fdm_printing` when one is requested, and the frontend neither shows the printer tab nor the wizard steps for them. Spool logic stays filament-only (grams).

## Generic printer topology

A printer profile attaches to an existing Machine. It contains ordered input units and slots, such as a direct feed or multi-material unit. Staff can explicitly mount and unmount spools. The assignment table retains mount history and enforces one open assignment per slot and spool.

The topology remains intentionally generic. It stores stable external identifiers without putting Bambu-specific semantics into Core. The H2D and X1C connectors map observational AMS unit/tray identifiers onto this topology. Exact-RFID auto-detection is disabled by default; when enabled on an assignment, an exact configured unit/tray plus an existing active NFC identifier may create or move only a connector-reconciled mount. Two consecutive tag-absent observations close the slot's open mount, a manual one included. Positive evidence never replaces a manual mount, and Core never creates a spool/tag from telemetry; and material, color, `tray_uuid`, and remaining percentage are never identity or stock authority. Core performs no printer discovery, slicing, scheduling, or physical start/pause/stop operation.

Artifact filament quantities remain plans until a recorded run reaches a terminal outcome. The active run page can present `~used / planned` from parsed planned grams and live printer progress, but that approximation is display-only and never reaches the ledger. Successful runs may use complete slicer-estimated grams. Interrupted runs use the latest conservative parsed G-code checkpoint consistent with reliable layer/progress evidence; they never multiply total weight by MQTT percentage for inventory. Model, purge/loading, and unclassified extrusion remain separate provenance while inventory uses their defensible total. Missing density/diameter and missing slicer normalization data produce an unresolved estimate for staff correction rather than invented stock use. A later connector revision compensates once, while a staff correction remains authoritative.

## Print submissions and attempts removed

Print submissions, print attempts, filament declarations, printer-event reconciliation, and the printing-operation idempotency records were removed with their tables and `/print-submissions` and `/print-attempts` endpoints. Printer connectors report recorded runs, which staff attach to or create [jobs](machine-logbook.md#jobs-and-runs) from.

Normalized connector events use a versioned source ID and event key. Repeating identical facts is idempotent; reusing the key for different facts is rejected. A consumption report becomes the run's reported usage and is consumed when the run ends; a later revision replaces it, segments included, without double counting, keeping the source-event reference on the usage. A line fed by several spools is booked per segment on the spool each one resolved to at its start (see [Machine-reported material](machine-logbook.md#jobs-and-runs)). The inventory entries, pricing recalculation, and audits commit in one database transaction.

## Permissions and routes

The feature adds `filament_spools.manage` and `printer_slots.assign`. Services enforce these permissions; frontend gating is only presentation.

OpenAPI defines `/filament-spools` (with `retire`), material spool transfers, printer profiles/input units/slots and the spool part of the inventory recycle bin. All pages remain route-lazy-loaded.

`GET /filament-spools` leaves out spools of articles in the recycle bin. Its `search` is a case-insensitive substring match on the label, storage location, notes, acquisition reference, identifier values (tag IDs and similar), the article's display ID, name, type, colour and manufacturer, and the name of the machine the spool is mounted on.

Migrations `00032_filament_spools.sql` and `00033_printing.sql` contain downgrade guards. A downgrade stops when spool, printing, or version-2 financial snapshot history exists rather than silently discarding it. The downgrade of `00038_spool_fills_products.sql` restores the single-material spool model from each spool's latest fill; older fills with another material cannot be represented. The downgrade of `00056_inventory_articles.sql` refuses while part types, article variants, sale prices or purchase references exist and otherwise rebuilds one product per filament article that has a manufacturer and package size.
