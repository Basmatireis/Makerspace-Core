# Orders, payments, and external invoicing

Makerspace-Core records internal charges. It does not issue legally binding invoices. wiRef issues invoices through the external accounting process.

```text
Machine Job / manual service → immutable Order Item → Order
                                                    ├→ cash/card Payment + allocations
                                                    └→ wiRef request → approval → forwarded to wiRef (issued)
                                                                       → settling external Payment
```

## Domain and lifecycle

The `internal/orders` feature owns charge, settlement, and external-invoicing services and sqlc persistence. It calls the Machine Logbook's concrete transaction-aware job methods for anonymous checkout. HTTP transport types, domain types, and generated persistence rows remain separate.

An Order has a UUIDv7, a stable sequence-backed reference (`O-2026-000001`), EUR currency, an optional Person/Organization payer, and optimistic version. References use the UTC year and a global sequence; gaps, including rolled-back previews, are expected. The payer may differ from the Machine Job customer and operator.

Lifecycle is `draft → finalized → reversed` or `draft → cancelled`. Settlement is derived from charges, full-reversal adjustments, and signed immutable allocations: `unpaid`, `partially_paid`, `paid`, or `no_payment_due` for zero net charges (including reversed records). Draft items may be added, removed, refreshed, or replaced by new item rows. Removed rows remain visible to the API as history. Finalized payloads, item snapshots, payments, and financial evidence cannot be rewritten. A draft can always be finalized without payment (`POST /orders/{id}/finalize`, the full total stays outstanding and is paid later with "Record payment"). With `payments.record` and `payments.read` the Order page additionally offers "Finalize and take payment" (checkout) as the primary action next to the plain "Finalize" button.

Manual items contain a description, positive quantity, unit, nonnegative unit price, and rounded line amount. Quantity and unit price have up to six decimal places; money has two. Go uses exact decimal arithmetic, PostgreSQL uses numeric, and the API uses decimal strings. Each manual line is rounded half-up to cents before summing Order totals.

**Inventory items.** The item kind `inventory_item` sells an inventory article (`materialId`, quantity, optional `unitPrice`). The item snapshots the article name as description and the article unit, and keeps a detachable `sourceMaterialId`; it is priced and rounded like a manual line. The unit price defaults to the article's sale price (`422 sale_price_required` when neither is given); inactive articles answer `409 article_inactive`. Adding needs `orders.write` and `inventory.read`; editing, refreshing and removal behave like manual items, and replacement drafts copy active inventory items. Order payloads expose no cost or valuation data.

Stock moves only with the Order lifecycle (migration 00057). Finalization (also finalize-with-payment, checkout and replacement commit) books one ledger row `order_sale` per active inventory item in the same transaction, from the article's unassigned (loose) stock at the current weighted-average cost, locking the articles in UUID order. If loose stock does not cover a line, the whole finalization fails with `409 insufficient_stock` (`details.materialId`, `details.available`) and nothing is written; stock that sits on a spool does not count. Reversal, and the release of the original by a committed replacement, book `order_sale_reversal` rows that return exactly the quantity and inventory value the sale removed to loose stock; the replacement consumes again when it is finalized. Cancelled drafts never touch stock. Retries with the same `operationKey` return the first result, and partial unique indexes allow one sale and one reversal per item. The order permissions authorize the consumption; `inventory.manage` is not needed. The material and spool ledgers show the order reference of these rows (linked with `orders.read`), and the 30-day `recentConsumption` of the article includes sales net of reversals. Audit events stay the order events (`order.finalized`, `order.reversed`) plus the ledger audit `inventory.order_sale` / `inventory.order_sale_reversal` with field names only.

**Inventory items in the UI.** On a draft, "Add item" asks what to add: a finished job of the order's customer that is on no order yet (with `machine_jobs.read`), something from inventory (with `inventory.read`), or a custom line. The inventory dialog searches active articles by article number and name, shows their loose stock and their price per pricing group, prefills the unit price with the price of the customer's group when the customer is an organization with its own group, and otherwise leaves the unit price empty so the server charges the price of the customer's pricing group. A refusal `422 article_price_missing` is shown as "No price for this customer" naming the group, with a link to Pricing (Articles sold from stock). The dialog shows the line amount (rounded half up to cents) and warns, without blocking, when the quantity exceeds the loose stock.

**Lists and pages.** The Sales & payments navigation group holds Orders · Payments · Pricing; the former overview at `/sales` redirects to `/orders`, and the breadcrumb "Sales & payments" links to Orders. The Price lookup is an action on the Pricing page. The Orders list has the tabs **Open · Needs attention · Paid · Cancelled** with their counts (`?section=`; see [Needs attention](#needs-attention)). It flags orders that need attention in the column before the row menu (the reasons in the tooltip). Each row ends in an overflow menu with the actions that are not on the order page: **Edit customer** and **Cancel draft** (drafts, `orders.write`), **Create replacement draft** (finalized or reversed orders not yet replaced, `orders.write`) and **Correct order…** (the same orders, `orders.reverse`); the selected order's panel offers the same menu next to "Open order", and the dialogs are those the order page used. From 1056 px on, the reference or a row click selects the order into a panel next to the list (`?order=` in the URL) with customer, status, total, outstanding, paid, invoice, items and payments; narrower screens open the order page. The order page header has **Edit order** on drafts (customer), **Print receipt** once a payment counts, the invoice action, and the primary action (Finalize and take payment, Record payment, Commit replacement…); it has no overflow menu. It shows the attention reasons as a notice (with "Refresh prices from the jobs" on drafts whose jobs changed), fact tiles, the Items and Payments tables, the invoice section, and Balance and Customer cards. Stock lines show a "Stock" tag and link to the article page for users with `inventory.read`; quantity and price stay editable on drafts like manual items. A finalization refused with `409 insufficient_stock` (finalize, checkout, replacement commit) is shown as "Not enough loose stock for <article>: <available> <unit> available." with a link to the article.

Job items preserve machine/type labels, UTC interval and exact duration, outcome, typed material usages, pricing group/rate evidence, calculated/override/effective amounts, override reason/time, and capture time. The snapshot has an explicit schema version and excludes customer/operator names, operational notes, external ingest metadata, addresses, and contact information. Source links are nullable. Job claims prevent simultaneous active charges; replacement drafts may provisionally reuse their original Order's jobs. A job item counts as outdated (`sourceChanged`, order attention `source_changed`, job billing state `order_outdated`) only when something it is billed for changed after capture: runs and usages, machine, recipient or pricing group, price calculation or adjustment, outcome, title, deletion or restore. The job carries a `billing_revision` for this, advanced only by such changes (migration `00123`; the job `version` stays the optimistic-lock counter), and the item stores the revision it captured. Notes, the operator and the signage flag never mark an order outdated. Finalization rejects a source whose billing revision changed after capture until the draft snapshot is refreshed.

Operational Machine Job corrections remain available after Order finalization, including compensating stock corrections. Order detail and Job detail show changed-source state. Correcting the financial charge requires explicit full reversal/replacement; it never recalculates the original Order silently.

## Needs attention

Every order carries `attention` (`{code, message}`, empty when nothing needs someone): `balance_overdue` (a finalized order still has an unpaid balance more than 14 days after it was finalized), `source_changed` (an active job item's job changed after it was charged) and `invoice_request_open` (the active wiRef invoice request is a draft, awaits approval or awaits the confirmation of its cancellation). The flags are derived when the order is loaded, for lists and details alike.

The **Needs attention** tab of the Orders list shows every order with at least one of these reasons, plus two the list derives itself: **partly paid** (a finalized order with `settlementState` `partially_paid`) and **old draft** (a draft created more than 7 days ago). Its filters are **Needs attention** (Outstanding for over two weeks = `balance_overdue`, Changed since billed = `source_changed`, Invoice awaits approval = `invoice_request_open`, Partly paid, Old draft; `?attention=`), Invoice and Customer type; switching to the tab clears the Customer and Created filters of the other tabs. The row flag lists the derived reasons too. With `machine_jobs.read`, a warning above the table, only in this tab, says how many finished jobs are on no order, names their job IDs and the sum ready to bill, and opens the Jobs ready to bill review ("Review jobs"). Without any order to show the tab says "Nothing needs attention right now."

## Replacement and payment allocation

V1 supports whole-Order reversal and linked replacement, and whole-payment reversals. It does not implement item-level credits, partial refunds, wallets, account credit, or overpayment. Creating a replacement copies active manual items and captures current source-job facts. Edit that draft, then commit through the original Order. One original has at most one committed successor; subsequent corrections replace the successor.

Replacement locks both Orders in UUID order and atomically releases original settlement, appends a full charge adjustment and its item evidence, releases job claims, finalizes the replacement, transfers remaining cash/card allocations, and records any explicitly supplied payment reversals/new receipts. Both versions must match. Transferring an allocation creates negative/positive immutable allocation entries with one operation identifier. It leaves the original Payment and cash/card journal totals unchanged.

If remaining receipts exceed the new charge, the operation fails. Explicitly reverse whole receipts and, where necessary, record a new receipt in the same replacement operation. No credit balance is created. Standalone reversal of an anonymous Order's payment must include a replacement receipt that preserves full settlement, or staff must reverse the whole Order instead.

Payments have stable `P-2026-000001` references suitable for writing into the physical cashbook. Cash, card, and externally confirmed settlement are distinct. Named Orders allow partial and multiple receipts; mixed cash/card is supported. Card receipts require a terminal/provider and unique confirmed transaction reference. Immutable reversals distinguish actual refunds, recording errors, and external accounting corrections. Recording-error reversals use the original effective occurrence time; an actual refund records its occurrence time. The digital journal reports recorded activity, not the complete physical cash balance. Its date bounds are inclusive `from`, exclusive `to` in UTC; method filters affect entries while totals show all methods in that period.

Commands use caller-generated UUIDv7 `operationKey`s. The service serializes retry keys before checking versions and persists only the fingerprint, actor, operation kind, and result identifiers. Reusing the same key/body/actor returns the existing result; different input returns `409 idempotency_conflict`. Authorization is checked again on retries. Cash/card reference uniqueness also protects against recording an already recorded terminal transaction with another key. Browser retry keys live only in memory; after navigation/reload reconcile against Payments and the terminal reference before recording again. No private query data is persisted in browser storage.

## Counter sales

There are no counter sales. Every Machine Job has a customer (Person or Organization) and every sale goes through an ordinary Order. The `/counter-sales` endpoints were removed; Orders that were created that way remain ordinary finalized Orders (the `anonymous` customer kind and its database checks stay for them). A terminal payment is an external fact. PostgreSQL rollback cannot undo it. If recording fails after a successful terminal payment, retry the same operation and reference without charging again; never assume a database failure refunded it.

## wiRef workflow

A separate ExternalInvoiceRequest has a `W-2026-000001` reference and the states `draft → submitted → issued`, plus `issued → cancellation_requested → cancelled`. `submitted` is shown as "Awaiting approval"; `issued` means "approved and forwarded to wiRef". The former `ready` state is gone.

- **Draft** (`external_invoice_requests.manage`): staff create the request for a finalized, named-customer Order that charges something, edit the recipient and service details, and **submit** it once the readiness checklist is complete (recipient name, billing address, country, ordered service period, description; organizations additionally a contact name and, when `organization_invoicing_requirements.require_purchase_order_reference` says so, a purchase order reference; the configuration is captured at submission). Staff may cancel a draft.
- **Awaiting approval** (`external_invoice_requests.approve`): only approvers edit the details (every save is audited), **return it for correction** (a comment is required and stored as a `returned` request event with its author, so the draft can show it), cancel it locally, or **mark it forwarded to wiRef**. Forwarding re-checks the checklist.
- **Issued**: the wiRef invoice number is optional. Forwarding may carry it; otherwise add it later with `reference-corrections` (event `reference_added`). A later change is a correction and needs a reason (`reference_corrected`).
- **Cancellation** of an issued request needs external evidence: `request-cancellation`, then `confirm-cancellation` with reconciliation kind, reference and date.

An invoice can be requested whatever the payment status. The request records `alreadyPaid`, the cash and card received for the Order; it follows the Order's cash/card payments and refunds while the request is a draft and is frozen from submission on. From `submitted` on (including `issued` and `cancellation_requested`) new cash/card payments and cash/card reversals on that Order are refused with `409 invoice_amount_frozen`; returning the request to the draft or cancelling it allows them again.

**Issued means paid.** In the same transaction as the forward transition, a payment of method `external` ("Invoice (wiRef)", source `wiRef`, reference = the request reference) is recorded and allocated to the Order for its open balance, so the Order is settled. If cash/card already covered the whole Order, no payment is created. Manually recording an `external` payment is refused with `409 invoice_settles_balance`; there is no bank-transfer recording. The settling payment is created on the strength of `external_invoice_requests.approve`.

Confirming the external cancellation reverses the settling payment (a reversal with reason kind `external_correction`, carrying the confirmation reference and date) in the same transaction and reopens the balance. Because that returns money, it additionally requires `payments.read` and `payments.reverse` when such a payment exists. Before Order reversal/replacement, cancel the request this way. Externally controlled payments are never transferred internally to a replacement. Core does not issue invoices; wiRef does. Core mails the invoice data to wiRef when a request is forwarded (see below); the wiRef invoice number is still recorded by hand.

### Delivery to wiRef

When a request is forwarded (`submitted → issued`), Core mails the invoice information to the wiRef address configured under **Settings › Sales & invoicing › Invoicing** (permission `invoicing.settings.manage`; `master` has it through the registry, existing Roles do not). The mail is the shared branded HTML layout with a PDF attachment (`invoice-request-W-2026-000001.pdf`) that carries the same data: recipient, address, optional contact person, purchase order reference, service period and description, the line items with quantity, unit price and amount, the total (with already paid and amount to invoice when cash or card was received), the payments and the approver. The payments are those that still count towards the Order (net allocation above zero, so refunded or corrected ones are left out), oldest first, each with its `P-…` payment ID, date, method (Cash, Card, or wiRef invoice for the settlement created by forwarding) and the amount it contributes to the Order; payer, recorder and card terminal references are not included.

Sending happens **after** the forwarding transaction commits and holds no lock; a delivery problem never undoes the forwarding. The outcome is stored per request in `external_invoice_request_deliveries` and returned as `wirefDelivery` (`status` `not_configured`, `sent` or `failed`, `attemptedAt`, `failureCode`), with its own audit event `external_invoice_request.wiref_delivery_<status>`. `not_configured` means sending was switched off or had no address; the invoice section then links to the settings. Failure codes are `mail_disabled` (SMTP switched off), `send_failed`, `render_failed`; neither addresses nor message bodies are logged or stored. An approver (`external_invoice_requests.approve`) can **Resend to wiRef** for an issued request at any time, for example after a failure or once the settings are complete; the request version does not change.

Settings: enabled flag, wiRef address, up to five CC addresses, an optional Reply-To address, mail subject, introduction and closing text, PDF title, introduction and closing text, and toggles for the optional fields (contact person, purchase order reference, service period, service description, approver). Recipient, address, items and totals are always included. Settings carry a version like the other settings (`409 stale_write`), every change is audited (`invoicing.wiref_settings_updated`), and a **test mail** with fictitious data goes to the saved addresses (subject prefixed `[TEST]`, available while sending is off). The settings screen previews the mail in a sandboxed frame and downloads a sample PDF; both render unsaved text.

Core does not receive mail. Replies from the accounting office go to the Reply-To address when one is set (migration 00064), otherwise to the sender address of the email delivery settings. The Reply-To address is a header only and never receives the message itself.

Texts may contain these placeholders and nothing else. Replacement is a single pass of plain string substitution over this whitelist (no template engine; a value that looks like a placeholder is not expanded again), mail HTML escapes every value, and unknown or malformed `{{...}}` is rejected on save with `422 wiref_template_invalid`. Placeholders of switched-off optional fields are empty.

| Placeholder                    | Value                                                                               |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `{{organization}}`             | Recipient name                                                                      |
| `{{contact_person}}`           | Contact person (empty when switched off)                                            |
| `{{purchase_order_reference}}` | Purchase order reference (empty when switched off)                                  |
| `{{request_number}}`           | Invoice request number, `W-2026-000001`                                             |
| `{{order_number}}`             | Order number                                                                        |
| `{{amount_to_invoice}}`        | Amount to invoice in EUR (order total minus cash and card received), `EUR 1,234.50` |
| `{{approved_by}}`              | Approver (empty when switched off)                                                  |
| `{{approved_on}}`              | Approval date, `DD.MM.YYYY` (Europe/Vienna)                                         |

Up to release 0.8.4 the placeholders had German names (`{{organisation}}`, `{{kontaktperson}}`, `{{bestellreferenz}}`, `{{anforderungsnummer}}`, `{{auftragsnummer}}`, `{{summe}}`, `{{freigegeben_von}}`, `{{datum}}`, in the order of the table). Migration 00066 rewrote the stored texts token by token and moved the settings version on. The old names are not accepted when saving, so stored texts only ever use the list above; configuration import renames them, so files exported by 0.8.3 and 0.8.4 still import.

The PDF is generated in pure Go with `github.com/go-pdf/fpdf` (MIT) using the built-in Helvetica font in cp1252, which covers German umlauts, ß and the euro sign; characters outside that set are replaced. The header follows the application header: the compact logo on the left (the bundled default or an uploaded PNG or JPEG; SVG logos are omitted), beside it the legal organization name in capitals above the display name. The backend embeds a copy of the default logo (`backend/internal/branding/documentmark/`); a test fails when it differs from `frontend/public/brand/htumkr-symbol.png`.

Recipient details are stored separately from the request header and stay editable while the request is a draft or awaiting approval (the item summary and `alreadyPaid` never change after submission); they freeze when it is issued. The UI exposes the purchase order configuration on the organization page (Directory › Organizations). Organization address and contact are stored on the organization (see [Machine logbook](machine-logbook.md)) and prefill the recipient details.

**List search.** `GET /orders` `search` is a case-insensitive substring match on the order reference, the customer name captured with the order and the reference of the order it replaces. `GET /external-invoice-requests` `search` matches the request reference and the reference of its order. The Orders page and the Cashbook tab of the Payments page filter in the browser: an order matches its reference, customer, status label, the references of the order it replaces or is replaced by, its invoice request reference and state, and the payment wording ("Paid in full", "No payment due", "outstanding"); a cashbook line matches its reference, order reference, customer, kind (Receipt, Refund, Correction, Cash withdrawal, Cash deposit), reason, reversed payment reference, external source and reference, method and who recorded it. The "Ready to bill" dialog matches customer, job name, job display ID and machine name.

**Cancelled requests stay visible.** An Order exposes its `active*` request (the one that is not cancelled) and `latestExternalRequestId/Reference/State`, the most recently created request in any state. After a cancellation the active request is empty and the latest one is the cancelled request, so the Order page keeps showing it with its state, cancellation reference and history, and offers "Request invoice again" (a new request that `supersedes` it). All requests of one Order, latest first and including cancelled ones, are listed by `GET /external-invoice-requests?orderId=...`; this needs `external_invoice_requests.read` like every request read, so the filter widens nothing. The Orders list filter "Invoice" has the value `cancelled` for Orders whose latest request was cancelled.

## Receipts

An Order that is finalized and has received payments (paid or partially paid) can print a receipt: "Print receipt" on the Order page opens `/orders/:id/receipt`, and on a payment page `/orders/:id/receipt?payment=:paymentId` shows only that payment. The route is lazy and needs `orders.read` and `payments.read`; the actions are hidden without them and for refund or correction lines. The page shows the makerspace name and logo from the branding settings, the Order reference, the date of the latest payment, the customer's display name, the items with quantity, unit price and amount, the total, the payments that still count (method, date, `P-…` reference, amount), the amount still outstanding, and a note when a wiRef invoice settled the Order. "Print" calls `window.print()`; print CSS hides the application header, navigation and page header.

A receipt has **no number of its own**: it is identified by the Order reference and the payment references, and it states that it is not an invoice. Receipt numbering and PDF export are plan item D5.

**Payments page.** `/payments` has the section tabs **Cash register** (default, needs `cash_movements.read` besides `payments.read`) and **Cashbook** (`?tab=cashbook`); switching tabs resets search, filters and selection. Both tabs share the period filter in the table's filter panel — All time (default, unbounded), Today, This week (from Monday), This month, Custom range… (a dialog with From and Through as whole days, From not after Through) — kept in `period`, `from` and `to`. The active period is a removable tag at the start of the toolbar (with the other applied filters) and a note above the content ("**This month** · 1 – 3 Nov 2026"). The ghost **Export** button (needs `cash_movements.read`) opens "Export cashbook": From/Through (prefilled from the period), Contents (Cash register only / All payments) and Format (PDF / CSV for Excel); it downloads `GET /payments/cashbook-export` through the generated client.

*Cash register* reads `GET /payments/cash-journal` (newest first, paged on the server, `search` and the Kind filter Receipt, Refund, Correction, Withdrawal, Deposit, Count sent along). The table has Time, Type, Reference (the P-/C-/K- reference above the order and customer, the reason and note, or what a count found), In, Out, Balance (the balance after the entry; a count shows its difference tag) and By (hidden below 42 rem), and a head row per day with "Saturday 31 October · opening balance €77.10" and the day's closing balance. From 1056 px on the panel next to it shows "Expected in the register" at the end of the period with the derivation (Cash receipts, + Deposits, + Count surpluses, − Refunds, − Payment corrections, − Withdrawals, − Count shortfalls, = Expected; the count lines only when counts found something, each label with a tooltip that explains it), **Count**, **Withdraw** and **Deposit** (`cash_movements.record`; icons only when the panel is narrower than 21 rem), the last count (difference tag, counted, expected then, when, by; "Not counted yet." without one) and the period's facts (cash in with receipts and deposits, cash out with refunds or corrections and withdrawals, net, counts). A row (or its reference) shows that entry in the panel instead (`?entry=`), with Go to order and Show receipt for payments; clicking it again or Close returns to the register. Below 1056 px the register summary sits above the journal and a payment row opens the payment page. **Count the register** takes the counted amount and the counting time (not in the future), asks `GET /payments/cash-register` with `to` just after that time for the expected amount, shows expected, counted and the difference, and for a difference asks for a reason (and a note for Unknown and Other); it sends the shown `expectedAmount`, so a register that changed meanwhile is refused instead of booked.

*Cashbook* shows the tiles Net takings, Cash, Card and Invoice (wiRef) with receipts and refunds or corrections, and every payment and cash movement of the period (client-side search and the filters Method, Kind and Customer) with Time, Payment, Order and customer ("Not tied to an order" with reason and note for movements), Method, Amount (negative in red), Recorded by and a row menu (Open payment, Open order, Show receipt, Refund or correct…). A row opens the payment page.


## Cash register and cash movements

The expected cash in the register counts **cash only**: cash receipts minus cash refunds and corrections, minus withdrawals, plus deposits, plus or minus count corrections, all recorded up to (excluding) the `to` instant of `GET /payments/cash-register`. Card and external (wiRef) payments, and their reversals, never change it. The register response gives the derivation (`receipts`, `refunds`, `corrections` = what count corrections took out, negative for a surplus, `refundsAndCorrections`, `withdrawals`, `deposits`, `expected`) and the last count before `to`.

Cash movements (`GET/POST /cash-movements`) are withdrawals and deposits with amount, reason (`bank_deposit`, `safe`, `float`, `other`; `other` needs a note), note, occurrence time (not in the future), a `C-2026-000001` reference and the recording Person. A withdrawal cannot exceed what the register should hold at its own time and now (`409 insufficient_cash`); writers are serialized with an advisory lock so retries (caller `operationKey`) and the limit see a stable register. Reading needs `cash_movements.read`; recording needs `cash_movements.record` plus read; the register figures need `payments.read` and `cash_movements.read`. A movement carries `direction` (`in`/`out`) and a positive `amount`; kind `correction` (reason `count_difference`, with `cashCountId`/`cashCountReference`) is booked only by a count and cannot be posted.

### Counting the register

`POST /payments/cash-counts` records a count (`K-2026-0001`): the counted amount and when it was counted (not in the future, and after the latest count, else `409 cash_count_out_of_order`). Under the register lock the server computes what the register should have held at that moment (everything up to and including `countedAt`) and stores it with the difference. A difference needs a reason — `counting_error`, `change_given_wrong`, `receipt_not_recorded`, `unknown` or `other` (the last two with a note) — else `422 cash_count_reason_required`; it books one correction movement with the signed difference at the counting time, referencing the count, in the same transaction, so the expected cash follows what is really there. A matching count books nothing and keeps no reason. The client may send the `expectedAmount` it showed; when that changed meanwhile, nothing is booked (`409 cash_expected_changed`). A backdated shortfall that later withdrawals already exceeded is refused (`409 cash_count_inconsistent`). Retries with the same `operationKey` return the recorded count. `GET /payments/cash-counts` lists them newest first. Counting needs `payments.read`, `cash_movements.read` and `cash_movements.record`; listing needs the two read permissions. Audit: `cash_count.recorded`, and `cash_movement.recorded` for the correction.

**Editing and deleting cash entries** needs `cash_movements.manage` (granted to no role by default; `master` has it), plus `cash_movements.read`, and for counts `payments.read`. `PATCH /cash-movements/{movementId}` changes the amount, time, reason or note of a withdrawal or deposit (omitted fields keep their value, the kind never changes); `DELETE /cash-movements/{movementId}` deletes it for good. A count correction belongs to its count and is changed or removed only with it (`409 cash_movement_is_correction`). `PATCH /payments/cash-counts/{countId}` changes the counted amount, counting time, reason or note; the server recomputes the expected amount at the (new) time without the count's own correction, the difference and the correction movement, which it creates, moves, changes or removes in the same transaction. The rules of recording apply (a reason for a difference, an omitted reason keeps the stored one, a note for `unknown` and `other`, a matching count keeps no reason, optional `expectedAmount` → `409 cash_expected_changed`), and a count may only move strictly between its neighbours (`409 cash_count_out_of_order`). `DELETE /payments/cash-counts/{countId}` deletes the count and its correction. Every body carries `expectedVersion` (`409 stale_write`); `CashMovement`, `CashCount` and the journal entries of movements and counts carry `version` and `updatedAt`.

A count anchors the register: from it on the register holds what was counted. So every edit or delete recalculates, under the register lock and in its transaction, the counts from the earliest moment it touched on (expected amount, difference, correction) until the first count after the change that needs nothing; a matching count that now differs keeps no reason until someone edits it (`reason` is null with a non-zero difference), and a count whose difference disappears loses its reason and correction. A change is refused with `409 cash_balance_negative` when the register would then hold less than nothing after any later entry (a dip that was already there and is not deepened does not block). Audit: `cash_movement.updated` / `cash_movement.deleted` and `cash_count.updated` / `cash_count.deleted` with the reference and the previous kind, amount, time and reason as metadata (never the note), `cash_count.recalculated` for counts that followed, and the correction's own movement events. In the Cash register tab the entry panel of a withdrawal, deposit or count shows **Edit** (the record dialog in edit mode, titled "Edit withdrawal", "Edit deposit" or "Edit count", primary "Save"; an untouched time keeps the stored instant with its seconds) and **Delete** (a confirmation naming the reference and amount, for a count saying that its correction is removed too) for people with `cash_movements.manage`; edited entries show when they were last edited.

### Journal

`GET /payments/cash-journal` is the register's journal: every cash receipt, refund and correction of a cash payment, withdrawal, deposit, count correction and count, with amount in or out and the balance after it (a count changes nothing; its correction follows it at the same instant). `from` and `to` are whole days in the makerspace time zone (`MAKERSPACE_TIME_ZONE`), both inclusive; `kind` (repeatable), `search` (references, order references, reasons, notes and, with `orders.read`, customer names) and `direction` (`asc` chronological by default, `desc` newest first) filter and order the paged items. The balance always counts from the first entry ever recorded, whatever the filters. Besides the items the response carries `days` (opening and closing balance and cash in/out of each day on the page), `openingBalance` of the range, `register` (expected cash at the end of the range with its derivation and the last count before it) and `facts` (cash in with the number of entries, receipts and deposits; cash out with entries, refunds, corrections and withdrawals; net; number of counts). Customer names are order data and are returned only with `orders.read`. The figures of one response come from one read-only snapshot. The view `cash_register_entries` (migration `00101`) unites the entries.

### Cashbook export

`GET /payments/cashbook-export?from&to&contents&format` downloads whole days as an attachment. `contents=cash_register` (default) lists the cash entries with the balance carried forward from the opening balance and replaces the handwritten cashbook; `all_payments` adds card and wiRef payments, with the method in brackets and no balance. Counts are not lines; their corrections are.

- **PDF** (`format=pdf`, default): the HTU Kassabuch form (`Kassabuch_Vorlage`), drawn with fpdf to the template's measurements. Every page carries the HTU letterhead, footer and fold marks; the artwork is stored as outlines in `backend/internal/orders/kassabuch_letterhead.txt` and drawn once per document. The first sheet has the head block (Kassabuch; Studienvertretung / Studienlabor / Referat: Makerspace (Studienlabor); Wirtschaftsjahr: the year or e.g. 2026/27 with the period, plus "alle Zahlungen" for all payments), the row "Bestand/Übertrag von vorheriger Seite" with the opening balance, the header row Nr., Datum, Kassierer*in, Text, Einnahme, Ausgabe, 14 entry rows and "Summe/Bestand" with the balance after the sheet. Every further sheet has the carry row with the previous sheet's Summe/Bestand, the header row, 17 entry rows and Summe/Bestand. Sheets are always complete: unused rows stay empty, so a short export is one full sheet. Entries are numbered from 1 over the whole export. The last page is always the final sheet: "Gesamtsumme (aller Einnahmen & Ausgaben)" with both totals, "Anfangsbestand (Wirtschaftsjahr)" and "Endbestand (Wirtschaftsjahr)", and the signature fields "Name, Datum & Unterschrift" for Kostenstellenverantwortliche/r, Wirtschaftsreferent/in and Vorsitzende/r. For all payments the carry row and the two balance rows stay empty and Summe/Bestand sums both columns of the sheet. Texts read "Einnahme O-… · Kunde", "Rückzahlung O-… · Grund", "Korrektur …", "Entnahme · Grund", "Einlage · Grund", with the entry reference small below; long texts and cashier names take two smaller lines.
- **CSV** (`format=csv`): semicolon-separated, decimal comma, UTF-8 with byte order mark; columns `Date;Reference;Description;In;Out` plus `Balance` for the cash register, whose first row is the opening balance. Text that a spreadsheet would run as a formula is prefixed with a single quote.

An export holds at most 5000 entries (`422 cashbook_export_too_large`). It needs `payments.read` and `cash_movements.read`; customer names appear only with `orders.read`.

## Actor display names

Orders expose `createdBy`, payments `recordedBy`, cash movements and counts `recordedBy`, journal entries `recordedBy` and request events `recordedBy` as `{id, displayName}` (the Person id and "first last"). Nothing else about the person is exposed. The value is null once the person is deleted.

## Integrity, privacy, and permissions

Migration `00123` drops the former fulfilment mode (anonymous orders must still be fully settled when finalized) and adds the billing revisions. Migrations `00027`–`00029` add the domain (`00048` the approval states and `already_paid`, `00049` cash movements, `00100` cash counts and count corrections, `00101` the register view, `00102` versions and identity guards for editing movements and counts), remove disposable legacy `machine_jobs.billing_status`/`billing_reference`, and enforce deferred financial constraints. There is no migrated legacy billed/waived history. Database checks enforce distinct payer references, immutable payloads, unique active job claims/requests/receipts/replacements, fully allocated or fully reversed receipts, bounded settlement, full charge reversal, and anonymous completion. Deferred checks allow intermediate states inside an atomic checkout/correction but reject invalid commits. Nullable historical references may detach to NULL, never be reassigned on immutable records.

Financial mutations and minimal audit events commit together. Audit metadata contains identifiers/changed-field names, not copied names, addresses, contact channels, reasons, or service descriptions. Operations store no request/response bodies. Errors expose the stable application envelope rather than SQL diagnostics.

Permissions are `orders.read/write/finalize/reverse`, `payments.read/record/reverse`, `external_invoice_requests.read/manage/approve`, and `cash_movements.read/record`. Mutations require relevant read permission as well as the action permission; replacement also requires finalization and payment read/record, and supplied reversals require payment reverse. Organization requirements use `organizations.manage`. All are registered grants; no business role names authorize operations. Order readers see minimal active-request identifiers/state, but recipient/address details require external-request read permission.

Person/Account deletion detaches financial references without deleting Orders, Payments, allocations, adjustments, or external requests. Minimal Order customer-name snapshots and wiRef recipient snapshots remain separate retained data; deleting a Person does not erase those financial snapshots automatically. Owned child rows may cascade when their aggregate is deliberately purged; cross-aggregate financial references use SET NULL. There are no new financial deletion endpoints or retention scheduler.

Before implementing GDPR retention, define approved retention periods/legal bases independently for technical jobs, charge records, receipts, billing snapshots, audit, and idempotency evidence. Design coordinated snapshot erasure/anonymization and aggregate purges, preserved references/evidence, and actor attribution. Existing inventory consumption rows require a live usage link: a future job/usage purge must reconcile that constraint with its SET NULL foreign key. Jobs can retain technical facts while customer/operator links detach today. Test the resulting cleanup transactions against immutability/deferred constraints and populated downgrade protection. Do not treat the current immutable snapshots as permanently exempt from erasure.
